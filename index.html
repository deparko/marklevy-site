The problem

You've used ChatGPT. Some days it feels like a superpower. Some days it feels like the thing that quietly replaces you.

Meanwhile the questions pile up and nobody around you has real answers. Can client information touch these tools without breaching confidentiality? Which of the fifty AI products your inbox pitches every week actually matter? Is your competitor down the street already doing this?

You're not behind because you're not smart enough. Whether you work alone or run a firm of twelve, you carry a company's worth of liability with no IT department, no compliance officer, and no time to become a technologist. A course won't settle any of this, and neither will another demo. The question is where AI fits in your work and where it should stay out — and answering it takes someone who has looked hard at both.

What I help with

Automate the context. Keep the core.

We start with a map of how your work actually moves — from "yes" to "paid," with the waits timed. The waits are where the money leaks. Then the repeatable parts become routines: AI does the gathering and the first draft, drops the result in a folder you control, and waits for your approval. Some of the places that shows up:

  • Research and analysis. Reading a stack of source material, pulling out what matters, and showing its work so you can check it.
  • Writing and client communication. First drafts in your voice — letters, memos, proposals, follow-ups — that wait for your edit before anyone sees them.
  • Document-heavy work. Contracts, reports, filings, plans: summarized, compared, and checked against your own standards.
  • Knowledge management. Your notes, files, and past work made searchable, so the answer you gave three years ago is there when you need it again.
  • Meeting preparation. Everything you know about a client, gathered before the call instead of after it.
  • Tools, routines, and automation. Which subscription, configured how, on accounts you own — and a recurring job set up so it does its part and stops at your approval.
  • Private and local AI. When confidential work shouldn't touch a cloud service at all, and what to run instead.

Common questions

Is my client data safe with AI?
With consumer chatbots and default settings — often not, and that's the trap. There are configurations, business-grade agreements, and when necessary fully private setups where nothing leaves your control. Sorting this out for your specific obligations is the first thing we do.
Is this AI training? Will you teach me ChatGPT?
Training is part of every engagement, and it comes last. Anyone can show you what the tools do. The work is figuring out where they belong in your week, setting them up so confidential material stays where it should, and writing down enough of your judgment that the results sound like you. Then, yes — I train you on it, hands-on, until you run it without me.
I'm not technical. Is this going to be over my head?
No. You're elite at your profession; you don't need to become a technologist. Everything is translated into your world, and you'll never be handed a system you can't run.
Will AI replace what I do?
It will replace parts of what you do — mostly the parts your clients never paid for anyway. It cannot replace knowing what right looks like in your field. The professionals who win point AI at the busywork and keep their judgment in charge. That's the whole practice.
Do you build software? Will you run my systems?
Neither — and that's deliberate. I'm your advisor, not your IT department. Where a working routine is part of an engagement, I set it up on tools you already own and teach you to run it yourself. If your situation ever calls for real infrastructure, I'll spec it, help you choose who builds it, and stay on your side of the table while they do. You should never be dependent on your advisor to keep the lights on.
What tools will I need? What does the setup cost to run?
Usually one professional-grade AI subscription — roughly the cost of a nice dinner each month — that you own, on your own laptop. I'll tell you exactly which one and why, configure it for your business and your confidentiality obligations, and teach you to run it. No server room, no IT contract, no equipment.
Why aren't your prices on the site?
Because the right number depends on your business, and I'd rather quote you honestly than post a range you have to guess yourself into. What I will commit to publicly: every engagement is a fixed fee, agreed in writing before work starts. I don't bill hourly, I don't run a meter, and you'll know the full number before you decide anything. The first conversation is free and there's nothing to sign at the end of it.
Who is this NOT for?
Firms with an IT department. Anyone who wants to fully automate client work with no human review. Anyone shopping for the cheapest prompt list.
